<h2>📋 How VAT works for Meta Ads</h2>
<p>Advertising services are billed to German businesses by the Irish entity <em>Meta Platforms Ireland Limited</em>. As the recipient you become liable for the VAT under the <strong>reverse-charge procedure (§ 13b UStG)</strong>.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Save your VAT ID in the ad account</strong> (Business Manager → payment settings → tax information). Without a valid VAT ID, Meta may charge Irish VAT that you cannot deduct in Germany.</li>
  <li><strong>Check the invoice:</strong> net amount, reverse-charge note and your VAT ID.</li>
  <li><strong>Calculate the VAT yourself:</strong> 19% on the net amount, deductible as input tax in the same period if you are fully entitled.</li>
  <li><strong>Timing:</strong> the period in which the service was rendered counts, not the payment date.</li>
</ul>

<h2>⚠️ The 4 most common mistakes</h2>
<ol>
  <li><strong>No VAT ID in the account:</strong> Meta then charges Irish VAT, and fixing it later is painful.</li>
  <li><strong>Booking gross with 19% input tax:</strong> there is no German VAT on the invoice, so none may be booked.</li>
  <li><strong>Card statements instead of invoices:</strong> a bank or card record does not replace the invoice. Download invoices monthly under “Billing & payments”.</li>
  <li><strong>Several ad accounts, one booking:</strong> each ad account issues its own invoices; keep each document traceable.</li>
</ol>

<h2>🧾 Small businesses and partial deduction</h2>
<p>Under the <strong>small-business rule (§ 19 UStG)</strong> you still owe the reverse-charge VAT but cannot deduct it. With <strong>partly VAT-exempt turnover</strong> (e.g. medical professions) input tax is only partly deductible. Clarify this with your tax advisor before you scale spend.</p>

<h2>💡 Conclusion</h2>
<p>Three things make Meta Ads tax-simple: <strong>VAT ID in the account, net invoice, clean VAT return (fields 46/47/67)</strong>. This article does not replace individual tax advice.</p>
