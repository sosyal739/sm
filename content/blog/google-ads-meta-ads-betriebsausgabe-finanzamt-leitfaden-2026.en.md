<h2>🏢 Which German Taxes Are Reduced by Google & Meta Ad Spend?</h2>
<p>Digital advertising costs are recognized as fully deductible operating expenses (Betriebsausgaben) by the German tax office (Finanzamt). They lower your company's net profit, directly reducing:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Income Tax (Einkommensteuer):</strong> For sole proprietors, partnerships (GbR, OHG), and freelancers (Freiberufler).</li>
  <li><strong>Trade Tax (Gewerbesteuer):</strong> Reduces the commercial profit base assessed by local German municipalities.</li>
  <li><strong>Corporate Tax (Körperschaftsteuer):</strong> Lowers the statutory corporate tax burden for incorporated entities (UG, GmbH, AG).</li>
  <li><strong>Value-Added Tax (Umsatzsteuer):</strong> Handled neutrally under the Reverse Charge mechanism with zero cash outflow.</li>
</ul>

<h2>📥 Step-by-Step: How to Download Official Google Ads Invoices</h2>
<p>German tax auditors strictly reject credit card statements or bank notification emails. You must archive the official monthly VAT PDF invoice:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Log into your <strong>Google Ads account</strong>.</li>
  <li>Navigate to <strong>Tools & Settings</strong> (wrench icon) > <strong>Billing</strong> > <strong>Documents</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select the relevant billing month.</li>
  <li>Download the <strong>Tax Invoice (EU-Rechnung PDF)</strong>.</li>
  <li><em>Compliance Verification:</em> Ensure your company name, full German business address, and German VAT number (DE...) are printed correctly alongside the § 13b UStG Reverse Charge notice.</li>
</ol>

<h2>📥 Step-by-Step: How to Download Meta Ads (Facebook/Instagram) Invoices</h2>
<p>Meta handles its European advertising operations via Meta Platforms Ireland Ltd.:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Open <strong>Meta Ads Manager</strong> at <em>adsmanager.facebook.com</em>.</li>
  <li>Click on <strong>Billing & Payments</strong> in the left sidebar menu.</li>
  <li>Set the transaction date filter to the complete calendar month.</li>
  <li>Click <strong>Download All</strong> to export the itemized invoices and monthly statement as PDFs.</li>
</ol>

<h2>❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) — Deducting German Ad Expenses</h2>
<div class="space-y-4 my-6">
  <details class="group bg-slate-900/60 border border-slate-800 rounded-xl p-4 [&_summary::-webkit-details-marker]:hidden">
    <summary class="flex items-center justify-between cursor-pointer font-semibold text-white">
      <span>How are small business owners (Kleinunternehmer § 19 UStG) affected?</span>
      <span class="text-blue-400 group-open:rotate-180 transition-transform">▼</span>
    </summary>
    <p class="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-300 leading-relaxed">
      Although exempt from charging German VAT on sales, small business owners are still liable for 19% German VAT on cross-border EU services under § 13b UStG. They must pay this 19% to the Finanzamt without input tax deduction. However, both the net ad expense and the 19% tax paid are 100% deductible as operational business expenses in annual profit calculations (EÜR).
    </p>
  </details>
  <details class="group bg-slate-900/60 border border-slate-800 rounded-xl p-4 [&_summary::-webkit-details-marker]:hidden">
    <summary class="flex items-center justify-between cursor-pointer font-semibold text-white">
      <span>Why is VAT listed as 0% on my Google and Meta ad invoices?</span>
      <span class="text-blue-400 group-open:rotate-180 transition-transform">▼</span>
    </summary>
    <p class="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-300 leading-relaxed">
      This is standard EU cross-border B2B Reverse Charge billing. Because the services originate in Ireland and you provided a valid German VAT ID, tax liability transfers to the recipient in Germany. Your accountant reports it as both input tax and output tax simultaneously, resulting in zero net cash cost.
    </p>
  </details>
  <details class="group bg-slate-900/60 border border-slate-800 rounded-xl p-4 [&_summary::-webkit-details-marker]:hidden">
    <summary class="flex items-center justify-between cursor-pointer font-semibold text-white">
      <span>What should I do if Irish VAT (23%) was mistakenly charged?</span>
      <span class="text-blue-400 group-open:rotate-180 transition-transform">▼</span>
    </summary>
    <p class="mt-3 text-sm text-slate-300 leading-relaxed">
      Immediately update your billing profile to 'Business' and enter your German VAT ID (USt-IdNr.). Then open a support ticket with Google or Meta requesting retroactively corrected invoices. The German Finanzamt cannot refund Irish VAT paid by mistake.
    </p>
  </details>
